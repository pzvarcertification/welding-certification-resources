# EN 15085 Railway Welding Resources

Railway welding requires controlled processes, qualified personnel, suitable welding procedures, traceability and documented quality records. The EN 15085 standard series establishes requirements for welding activities related to railway vehicles and railway vehicle components.

For manufacturers working in the railway sector, understanding the applicable EN 15085 requirements is important before defining the certification scope.

## What Is EN 15085?

EN 15085 is a European standard series covering welding activities for railway vehicles and their components. It addresses the control of welding processes, personnel competence, welding coordination, production activities, inspection and technical documentation.

The applicable requirements depend on the manufacturer's products, welding activities, responsibilities and defined certification scope.

Manufacturers involved in railway vehicles, coaches, wagons, locomotives, bogies and welded railway components should determine which requirements apply to their production activities.

For detailed information, see the [EN 15085 Certification Requirements](https://pzvar.in/en-15085-requirements/) article.

## Key Areas Covered by EN 15085

Depending on the certification scope, railway manufacturers should review several areas of their welding operations.

### 1. Welding Coordination

Welding coordination is an important part of controlled railway welding production. Responsibilities should be clearly defined and assigned to appropriately qualified personnel.

The welding coordination function can involve reviewing welding procedures, monitoring production, maintaining qualification records, checking welding activities and ensuring that applicable requirements are followed.

### 2. Welding Procedures

Manufacturers need appropriate welding procedures for the welding activities included within their production scope.

Welding Procedure Specifications (WPS) define important production parameters and provide controlled instructions for carrying out welding operations.

Where procedure qualification is required, the Welding Procedure Qualification Record (WPQR) provides documented evidence that the welding procedure has been qualified for the applicable conditions.

### 3. Welder and Welding Operator Qualifications

Personnel carrying out welding activities should have qualifications applicable to the work they perform.

The qualification should be relevant to factors such as the welding process, material, thickness, welding position and other applicable parameters.

Manufacturers should also maintain appropriate qualification records and ensure that personnel qualifications remain valid for the work being performed.

### 4. Materials and Traceability

Material identification and traceability are important parts of controlled railway welding production.

Manufacturers should be able to connect relevant material information with production documentation, welding activities and inspection records.

A clear traceability system can include:

- Parent material identification
- Welding consumables
- Production records
- WPS and WPQR references
- Welder identification
- Inspection records
- Final quality documentation

### 5. Production and Inspection Controls

Welding activities should be carried out under controlled production conditions.

Depending on the defined scope, manufacturers may need appropriate inspection, testing and non-destructive testing arrangements.

Production records should provide evidence that the required welding and inspection activities have been completed.

### 6. Technical Documentation

Technical documentation forms an important part of the railway welding quality system.

Relevant documentation may include drawings, welding procedures, qualification records, personnel qualifications, material records, inspection results, NDT records and production documentation.

Maintaining clear and traceable records makes it easier to demonstrate that welding activities are controlled within the defined scope.

## EN 15085 Certification Scope

The certification scope should reflect the manufacturer's actual products and welding activities.

Before certification, manufacturers should identify:

1. Railway products and components manufactured
2. Welding processes used
3. Production locations
4. Welding coordination responsibilities
5. Applicable personnel qualifications
6. WPS and WPQR documentation
7. Material traceability arrangements
8. Inspection and testing activities
9. Production controls
10. Technical and quality records

A clearly defined scope is important because EN 15085 requirements can vary according to the products, welding activities and responsibilities covered by the manufacturer.

## EN 15085 Certification Levels

EN 15085 certification classification is related to the type and importance of welded railway products and the welding activities carried out by the manufacturer.

The applicable classification should be determined according to the manufacturer's products, welding work and relevant requirements rather than selecting a classification without considering the actual production scope.

Manufacturers should therefore establish the applicable certification scope before proceeding with assessment.

## WPS and WPQR in Railway Welding

WPS and WPQR documentation plays an important role in demonstrating controlled welding procedures.

A WPS specifies the production parameters and instructions applicable to a welding operation.

A WPQR provides documented qualification of the welding procedure where qualification is required.

The relationship between the drawing, material, WPS, welder, welding activity and inspection record should remain traceable through production.

## Railway Welding Quality and Traceability

A strong traceability system connects important stages of production:

Drawing → Material → WPS → Welder → Weld → Inspection/NDT → Final Record

This relationship allows manufacturers to maintain clear production evidence and demonstrate how welding activities were controlled.
